# Decisions

The settled meta-decisions for Phantom Island, with what each one means for the work. The
discussion that led to them is in [GAME_INCEPTION.md](GAME_INCEPTION.md); the story draft is in
[GAME_STORY.md](GAME_STORY.md). Add a line here whenever something is settled, and change an
entry rather than adding a contradicting one.

Settled on 2026-11-10 unless noted.

## Development staging

The full playable game comes first, with no AI. AI characters come second, after a side step
to build the base AI package from the `llm_lab` findings.

| Phase | Content | Ends with |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Complete game without AI** | A1 island skeleton (rooms, exits, map) · A2 scripted inhabitants (`ask <char> about <topic>`) · A3 discovery and shared journal · A4 mystery, gates, events, ending · A5 polish · playtest with the co-designers · fixes | A finished adventure the co-designers have played and approved |
| **B. Base AI package** | B1 package design, its interface taken from A2 · B2 implementation · B3 one character switched to AI | One AI character in the game; the game still decides which clues are revealed |
| **C. AI characters** | One character at a time, tested against playtest transcripts · free conversation · second playtest | The same adventure with AI inhabitants |
| **D. Growth** | More adventures, islands, modes | — |

Every stage ends with something playable from start to finish, tested with two players logged
in (two browser windows are enough).

## World and screen

Settled on 2026-11-11.

| # | Topic | Decision | What it means for the work |
|---|---|---|---|
| 13 | **Rooms** | 12 places: **Port** (Society Office, Quay, Market), **Ship** (Deck, Cabin), **Island** (Lagoon Beach, Village, Western Reef, Forest Path, Spring, Hilltop, Boat Cove), plus Limbo as the prologue. | The island layout in A1 is a first sketch, open to change. |
| 14 | **The ship: two rooms that travel** | **Deck** is for observing: sky, sea, birds, coastlines; `sail`, the noon sight and going ashore happen here. **Cabin** is for instruments and records: chronometer, chart table with the *Halcyon*'s chart and the ship's own track, water casks, books. (The `report` works anywhere: 29.) Longitude needs both: noon seen on deck, the chronometer read below (it really stayed below). **The Ship's voice is heard anywhere aboard** (2026-11-12): she answers `ask ship` on the Deck and in the Cabin, and is silent ashore. | The ship doesn't move between rooms; its Deck view and its `ashore` exit change with where the ship is (Quay, positions at sea, island anchorage). Places the ship can sail to are kept as data. The Ship is one character, found by `ask` from either ship room. Gates live in the destination rooms (e.g. the Western Reef checks the tide), so no exit can bypass them. |
| 15 | **Limbo is the prologue** | The game starts in Evennia's Limbo (`#2`), dressed as a title page: its description is the pitch, with one exit into the Society Office. | Limbo is not one of the 12 places and has no map; the Focus pane can show a title picture. |
| 16 | **Screen: three panes, maps switch by act** | **Main** (text scroll: descriptions, conversations, journal), **Map** (where am I?) and **Focus** (the current room's picture, or whatever is being examined: compass, letter, chart). The map switches with the act: a **port plan**, the **sea chart** (the *Halcyon*'s chart with the island where she put it and the ship's track; the true island appears once found) and an **island map**. | The picture-map feature is copied from `game/` and extended with the Focus pane. Pictures stay out of the text scroll. Everything shown in Map or Focus also exists as text, for telnet players. Placeholder pictures in A1; real art in A5. |
| 17 | **Nobody is left behind** (2026-11-11) | The ship won't sail while another connected player is still ashore. Players still in Limbo (the title page) haven't joined the expedition, so they don't count. | `sail` names who is still ashore and refuses. Follows from 6b: no player can be stranded by another. |
| 18 | **Late joiners** (2026-11-11) | **`begin` leads to wherever the expedition is.** While the ship is in port, `begin` goes to the Society Office as usual. While she is away, it puts the new player on the Deck, with a short in-story line (they were aboard all along, e.g. seasick below). The shared journal (A3) is how they catch up; the other player can guide them. Fresh runs (a reset world) stay possible. | Built in A3: the `begin` exit (`typeclasses/exits.py`) asks `world/ship.py` where to lead. |
| 19 | **Conversation** (2026-11-12) | `ask <character> about <topic>`, scripted in [characters.yaml](../world/content/characters.yaml). **Topic words are shared on purpose, replies are per character**: asking several witnesses about the same thing (*halcyon*, *compass*, *storm*...) and comparing is the historian's lesson. Every character has a polite fallback for unknown topics. `ask <character>` alone greets and suggests a few topics, and replies mark further topic words in white. A first version has 4–6 topics per character and about 10 for the Ship. | Each topic records its stance (truth, mistaken, hiding), the clue it reveals (A3) and, for hidden things, a `truth` reply with its conditions (A4). Rooms and objects don't answer `ask`; they answer `look` (details), `read` and `use`. `show <thing> to <character>` is left for A4. In phase B this topic list is what an AI character may reveal. |
| 20 | **Clue colour** (2026-11-12) | **Two colours carry meaning: white marks words worth following up (to ask about, to look at, or a command to try), yellow marks clues.** White covers looking too since A3, so players find the details of a room (`look beams`) the same way they find topics. Only the fact itself is yellow (in Tui's song, "made a new canoe with a white wing"), never a whole reply, and only true clues: mistaken or lying replies stay plain, so spotting them stays the player's job. A journal line always follows the yellow text, so the meaning never depends on colour alone (telnet without colour, colour-blind players). `help` explains the two colours. | Built in A3 with the journal. The YAML marks *meaning*, not colour: clue text is written `[[...]]`, and the code shows it in yellow (or plain, with hints off). The content check makes sure every reply with a `clue` has a marked phrase and no other reply does. The white topic words stay as `\|w...\|n` codes. A `hints off` setting that drops the yellow can come in A5 if the game feels too easy. **Cyan** (2026-11-17) is the report's verdict on an answer (proved, not proved yet, doesn't fit), on its own line so it stands out from the longer texts; not red, which would read as failing (29). |
| 21 | **Discovery and the journal** (2026-11-12) | **The journal is shared and kept in found order**, with who found what, where and from whom or what; a clue confirmed later by another source keeps one entry and lists the new source too (comparing sources, 19). After a playtest (2026-11-15) it shows one line per clue, and the sources only on request (`journal sources`): the list grew long, and the sources were often longer than the clues. No clue is marked as used or faded: most clues count again in the report, and deciding what is settled is the players' work; every connected player hears of a new clue. Grouping it under the three report questions is a strong hint, so it's left for A5, after a playtest. **Things can be carried**: the books and the chart travel ashore and show more of what their carrier looks at; the compass stays on Crane's stall and the chronometer stays in the Cabin. Carried things go back to their place when their carrier leaves the game, and when the server starts cold or the world is rebuilt while their carrier isn't being played (6b: no dead ends). **Hidden truths unlock from the journal**: a `truth` whose `needs` are clue ids is given as soon as those clues are found (Crane admits the truth once C2 is in the journal); flags such as Ama's `respect` come in A4. **Longitude takes two steps that two players can share**: the noon sight on the Deck sets a flag for the expedition, and the chronometer in the Cabin reads it. | Clues in [clues.yaml](../world/content/clues.yaml), things in [things.yaml](../world/content/things.yaml), details of places in `places.yaml` and `ship.yaml`. `look` (details and things), `examine`/`x` (a closer look), `read`, `use`, `journal`. Looking is private, reading and using are heard by the room. Our `examine` replaces Evennia's for players; builders keep `@examine` and `ex`. In A3 nothing waits for time: the tide, dusk and noon come in A4. |
| 22 | **Everything fits together** (2026-11-12) | **The game's distinctive feature: its details depend on each other, as in the real world**, and players who want to can find out *why*. Three layers: (1) **during play, facts not conclusions**: every clue is backed by background knowledge players can ask for (the Ship, the books, the instruments: how far seabirds fly, why no oak grows here, four minutes per degree), while the connection stays the player's to make; (2) **optional reflection** (A5, if the playtest asks for it): once related clues are both found, a quiet place such as the Cabin's chart table offers a *question* that links them, never the answer; (3) **"How it all fits"** after the `report` (A4): the chains from clues to answers, the numbers, and the real history with sources (9). No new all-knowing character: the Ship is the voice for deeper detail, and in phase C free conversation gives her the depth. | The chains of dependent facts are listed in [GAME_STORY.md](GAME_STORY.md), "Facts that depend on each other"; check it whenever a number, distance or direction changes. Later: each clue in `clues.yaml` names where its background lives, and the content check fails if it's missing; a test checks the arithmetic (minutes = 4 × degrees). The player-facing promise is in the guide and the Limbo pitch ("every detail has a reason"). |
| 23 | **Writing: richness of the plot, not the prose** (2026-11-13) | **Replies stay short; depth is one question further down.** About 30–60 words, the answer first and one concrete detail that is both vivid and a fact that fits the world. Richness comes from *connections* between the people, things and clues we have (Barnaby on Crane's compass, Fairweather's "good oak" and the village beams), not from more elements or more adjectives: in a game every noun is a promise players act on, so decoration is a false lead. Figurative language only in a character's voice, where it shows who they are. Each character has a written `voice`. | The rules are at the top of [characters.yaml](../world/content/characters.yaml), and each character's `voice` is a field there (also for the AI characters of phase B). A pass over all characters follows, one at a time, starting with Fairweather and Barnaby. The reasoning is in GAME_INCEPTION.md, "Richness: plot, not prose". |
| 24 | **Who commands the ship** (2026-11-13) | **The players lead the expedition and command the ship**; there is no captain character. The crew are background sailors who follow orders. The Ship has a mind of her own and gives advice (6b: hints that get more direct), but she sails where the players tell her. Deciding where to sail, against what the chart says, is the central choice of Act 2, so it stays the players' own. | Said by Fairweather (*mission*, *ship*), the Ship (*captain*) and the Deck description, which also points to `sail` and `ask ship`. |

| 25 | **Sailing orders and voyages** (2026-11-13, a first version to try in play) | **An order names a destination, and a voyage is told, not jumped.** From port the order is the position read from the chart (`sail to 14 south 40 west`; `sail to the island` also works); the Ship repeats it and explains why the course is due west (home and the island share the latitude: latitude sailing). At sea, where the numbers fail, orders are by direction (`sail west`) or name (`sail home`): the Act 2 choice reads differently from the first leg. A voyage is a short passage, a line every 3 real seconds with the ship's marker moving along the sea chart, and nothing to do or decide meanwhile. Wrong numbers get a gentle in-story correction, never a voyage to nowhere (6b). | Positions have `coords` and `orders` texts, routes `names`, the Ship's `order` and a `passage` in [ship.yaml](../world/content/ship.yaml). Between positions the ship is at `under_way`, whose texts the Deck, the Cabin and the instruments show. A voyage under way when the server restarts carries on. Educational steps (concepts, pictures, hints) may be added after playing it. |
| 26 | **At the empty position the players reason, the game measures** (2026-11-14, after a playtest where finding the island felt like guessing) | **Yellow marks the measurements, never the conclusion; wrong searches teach instead of being refused.** On arrival the Ship asks the players to check her reckoning with the sextant and the chronometer. Both measurements are yellow and confirm C4 (the ship is really at the chart's position), and the chronometer asks *which number would you trust?* instead of saying. C5 is measured only at the island. `sail` there lists no ready-made choice: any of the four directions, or home. A day north or south shows a changed latitude, a day east the waters already crossed on the way out; the only way on is back. Carrying things is taught: `take` and `pick up` work like `get`, and carryable things say so. | Settles the open question on C5 (it gave the answer away). Texts in [things.yaml](../world/content/things.yaml) (sextant, chronometer `at:` the new positions) and [ship.yaml](../world/content/ship.yaml) (`north_of_chart`, `south_of_chart`, `east_of_chart`). Water and the Ship's escalating hints come in A4. |
| 27 | **The notebook: ideas, beside the journal's facts** (2026-11-14) | **Each player has a notebook of ideas about how the world works; the journal stays the expedition's shared facts.** An idea (latitude, coral) belongs to an area (Navigation, Nature) and is learned at three levels, *heard*, *understood*, *used*; its entry is one paragraph that grows with the level, so competence shows as text, not as a status. Sources are recorded but shown only on request (`notebook sources`, with who else knows it). Progress counts ideas, once each ("Navigation: 2 of 9"), and only areas already started are shown. An entry states the idea, never what it means for the mystery. This replaces quizzes: *used* is the test. Character memory of players (Game 5) was dropped as a new axis for the scripts. | Ideas in [ideas.yaml](../world/content/ideas.yaml) (optional: the game works without it; 17 ideas in four areas, 2026-11-15), taught with `teaches: {idea: level}` beside `clue:` in the other content files; code in `world/notebook.py`, kept in the `Expedition` script by character, cleared by `startover`. Conversations and reading or using teach everyone in the room up to *understood*; *used* only the doer; looking only the looker. Routes teach too (`used` for whoever gave the order). New texts written for it: the Ship's *wind*, *horizon* and *witnesses*, Ama's *song*, coral in the animal guide. |
| 28 | **The task stands in the journal** (2026-11-15) | **The top of the journal states what the expedition is for, from the very beginning**: the Society's task and the three questions the `report` will ask (what happened to the *Halcyon* and where, why nobody found her, what became of her crew). After many moves players lose the purpose, and on the island there is nobody to ask; the journal is shared, works everywhere and is where they look back. It isn't a clue (clues are the mystery's facts, found and checked by the report). It only states the questions: grouping the clues under them stays out (21). Mrs. Fairweather's *mission* reply says the same questions in her words. | `task` at the top of [clues.yaml](../world/content/clues.yaml) (optional), shown by `journal` above the clues. |
| 29 | **The report** (2026-11-16) | **One question at a time, answered by picking from choices, anywhere, by any one player for the expedition; the full epilogue once all three are answered.** The choices are always the same, so the right one never gives itself away by appearing; it is accepted only once the journal holds its proof, and until then the game says what the Society will ask for. The wrong choices are what the witnesses believe (Barnaby's "sank in the storm") or a natural misunderstanding (the wrong number of the chart), so choosing tests understanding (6b) and is the historian's lesson (19). A wrong choice is turned down with a hint of what is missing, never a "you failed". There is no partial epilogue. | `report` works everywhere, not only in the Cabin (replaces the Cabin in 14). An answered question is marked under the task in the journal (28), and every connected player hears it. The content is in [report.yaml](../world/content/report.yaml); the code in `world/report.py`. The epilogue's pages are what happened, "How it all fits" (22), the real story, and the sources (9). Afterwards the world stays open: `report` shows the epilogue again, and `startover` begins a new expedition. |
| 30 | **Connection questions** (2026-11-17) | **Once the clues a connection needs are all in the journal, a question that links them comes up, never the answer.** It is shown once, to everyone in the finder's room, right after the journal line; `think` lists every open question, anywhere, and the journal's footer points to it. Not at the chart table (as 22 first suggested): on the island there is nobody to ask, the same reason as the task in the journal (28). A question leads one step further than the clues' journal lines, towards a question of the report, and points to where the background lives (the Ship, a book, a witness). | Seven connections in `connections` at the end of [clues.yaml](../world/content/clues.yaml); an entry of `needs` can be a list (any one of those clues). Worked out from the journal each time, so no new state. Code in `world/clues.py`, the command in `commands/discover.py`. |
| 31 | **Hints** (2026-11-17) | **`hint`, anywhere, gives the next step at three levels: a nudge, then more direct, then the exact command.** The level starts over when the expedition makes progress (a clue, a report answer). After 10 minutes of play without progress, a player's next command is followed by the first level, once per stretch without progress; pauses between commands count at most a minute, so breaks don't push. **Aboard, the Ship gives the hint; anywhere else, the journal does**, as the expedition's own thinking ("You leaf through the journal, and think."). Not a talking Island, Port or Map: an island that speaks would know the whole truth, the all-knowing character 22 rules out, and would bring magic into a story solved by measuring. Detecting players who read too fast was left out: hard to tell reliably, and pushed hints already reach players who are lost. | Situations in [hints.yaml](../world/content/hints.yaml), checked in order, the first that matches wins: conditions on the ship's position, clues and flags found or missing, report questions open. Every new gate or timed event adds its situation there. Code in `world/hints.py`; the clock is kept in the `Expedition` script and counted after every player command (`commands/command.py`). |
